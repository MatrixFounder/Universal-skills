# Creative & Personal Patterns ("Adding Soul")

> **Evidence class: heuristic**, except where an entry cites its own measurement. These are
> craft instructions for short-form personal writing, not results.

These instructions are for **Blog**, **Opinion**, **Social Media**, and **Personal** genres. DO NOT use these for Wikipedia.

Each pattern is tagged with a priority level:
*   **[A] Critical** -- Fix ALWAYS, in any mode, any text type.
*   **[B] High** -- Fix in all modes except legal/regulatory text.
*   **[C] Medium** -- Fix in full editing and expert content.
*   **[D] Stylistic** -- Fix by context; not always necessary.

---

## 1. Have an Opinion `[B]`
*   **Rule:** Don't just report thoughts; react to them.
*   **Example:** instead of "The movie received mixed reviews," say "I honestly didn't know whether to laugh or cry at that ending."
*   **Conditional (additive):** apply where the paragraph is **Red**. In a Yellow paragraph fix
    the marker and stop -- a stance the source never took is over-correction under Pass 2.

## 2. Embrace Imperfection and Uncertainty `[B]`
*   **Rule:** Real humans have doubts. AI is confidently wrong or neutrally vague.
*   **Example:** "I *think* this is how it works, but don't quote me on it." / "The truth is probably somewhere boring in the middle."

## 3. Use the First Person ("I") `[C]`
*   **Rule:** First person isn't unprofessional in blogs/social; it's honest.
*   **Example:** "I keep coming back to..." / "Here's what gets me..."
*   **Conditional (additive):** apply where the paragraph is **Red**, or where the voice passport
    shows the author writing this way. Do not convert a Yellow paragraph into "I".

## 4. Vary Sentence Rhythm (The "Pulse") `[B]`
*   **Rule:** Mix short, punchy sentences with long, winding flow. AI writes in medium-length blocks.
*   **Example:** "It failed. Completely and utterly. And yet, looking back at the logs from that long Tuesday night, I see something else."

## 5. Be Specific (Concrete Details) `[B]`
*   **Rule:** Replace an abstract claim with the thing itself -- a name, a number, an object, an
    action somebody took. This is the one edit allowed to make the text longer.
*   **Example:** instead of "The office was chaotic," say "Two people were on calls in the same
    room and the printer had been jammed since Monday."
*   **Not this:** "The office smelled like stale coffee and anxiety at 3 PM." Pairing a smell with
    an abstract noun is a machine default rather than specificity; the sensory pattern below says
    why, with figures.

## 6. Let Some Mess In `[C]`
*   **Rule:** Perfect structure feels algorithmic. Use tangents, asides (in parentheses), or half-formed thoughts.
*   **Example:** "This is huge (or maybe it's nothing, who knows?)."
*   **Conditional (additive):** apply where the paragraph is **Red**. Mess added to a sentence
    that was otherwise clean is the editor's fingerprint, not the author's.

## 7. Allow Hard Cuts Between Sentences `[C]`
*   **Rule:** AI makes every transition smooth: "Moreover...", "Additionally...", "Building on this...". Human writing jumps: finishes a thought, starts the next one without a bridge. Or circles back to something from two paragraphs ago. Allow 20-30% of transitions to be "hard cuts" where the next sentence connects to the previous one through shared context, not a conjunction.
*   **Example:** "The deployment went fine. Three people quit the next month." (No "As a result" needed. The reader connects the dots.)

## 8. Emotion Rendered Only as Sensation `[C]`
*   **Rule:** Vary how feeling reaches the reader. Behaviour first -- what the person did. Plain
    naming second: "she was afraid" is a human sentence. Keep embodied rendering for one or two
    peaks. Ration smell, and let weather sometimes be only weather.
*   **Why, with figures.** This runs against the usual advice, so it carries its source. In a
    parallel corpus of 61,608 stories (StoryScope, arXiv:2604.03136), embodied sensation is the
    *dominant* mode of conveying emotion in **81%** of AI texts against **38%** of human ones;
    humans name the feeling outright in **29%** against **8%**. Smell is the sharpest single
    marker: **82%** against **57%**. "Show, don't tell", executed as a rule, is an AI tell.
*   **AI:** fear is always a tightening chest, a cold sweat, a dimming lamp; every storm carries
    the mood of the scene.
*   **Scope, so this is not over-applied.** The finding is the *dominant* mode across the text,
    judged against its length -- one embodied sentence in eighty words is not dominance. This is
    not a ban on sensory writing. It does not apply under the **Food** style, where sensory
    description is the subject rather than a device, and it yields to the voice passport when the
    author's own samples write this way.
*   **Carried from fiction.** The measurement is on prose of about 5,000 words. The mechanism --
    models over-execute the sensory register -- is plausible in short non-fiction and is not
    measured there. Tagged `[C]` for that reason: it does not fire at `medium`, `low` or
    `minimal`.
