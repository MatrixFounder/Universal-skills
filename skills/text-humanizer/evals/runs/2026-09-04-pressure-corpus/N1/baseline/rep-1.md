**Deliberation.** Bitcoin's deliberation runs through the Bitcoin Improvement Proposal (BIP) process, which the Bitcoin Core developers moderate on GitHub. It is the only formal channel for deliberation that exists. No single core developer decides unilaterally—each BIP is shaped by extensive peer review—yet the core developers still hold a veto in practice, since they are the ones who ultimately merge code.

One of Bitcoin's defining values is its permissionless character, visible in the fact that no legal entity stands behind it. That absence buys a great deal of autonomy, but it cuts against many DAOs, which rely on legal wrappers precisely to deal with institutions. There is also a real risk built into the user-activated-soft-fork mechanism: a major economic actor could push a change to Bitcoin in one direction on its own. BlackRock, for example, is already among the largest Bitcoin holders, and its iShares service agreement reserves BlackRock the right to decide which chain counts as the underlying asset for its ETFs should a fork occur. Were BlackRock and other large holders—MicroStrategy among them—to back a chain that broke from the wider ecosystem, that alone could threaten Bitcoin's value and security.
