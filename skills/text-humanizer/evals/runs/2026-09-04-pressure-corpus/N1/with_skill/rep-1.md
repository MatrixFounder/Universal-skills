**Deliberation.** Bitcoin's main deliberative mechanism is the Bitcoin Improvement Proposal (BIP) process, moderated by the Bitcoin Core developers on GitHub, and it is the only formal one available. Core developers do not decide unilaterally; peer review shapes each BIP. They still hold a veto, however: integrating the code is ultimately their call.

Bitcoin has no legal entity behind it, and that is what makes it permissionless. The autonomy comes with a tradeoff: many DAOs rely on legal wrappers to interface with institutions, an option Bitcoin lacks by design. The user-activated-soft-fork mechanism carries its own risk: major economic actors could push a change to Bitcoin in a single direction. BlackRock, already one of the largest Bitcoin holders, illustrates the stakes: its iShares service agreement states that BlackRock itself decides which chain counts as the underlying asset for its ETFs if Bitcoin forks. Should BlackRock and other major holders, such as MicroStrategy, back a chain that splits from the broader ecosystem, Bitcoin's value and security could be at risk.
