**Deliberation.** The Bitcoin Improvement Proposal (BIP) process, run by the Bitcoin Core developers on GitHub, is Bitcoin's only formal mechanism for deliberation. Core developers do not decide unilaterally; extensive peer review shapes each BIP. They do hold a veto, though: they decide, in the end, which code gets merged.

A core value of Bitcoin is its permissionless nature: no legal entity stands behind it. That yields real autonomy, but it creates a tension: many DAOs adopt legal wrappers specifically to deal with institutions. The user-activated-soft-fork mechanism carries its own risk: a major economic actor could redirect Bitcoin unidirectionally. BlackRock now counts among Bitcoin's largest holders. Its iShares service agreement gives it sole authority to decide which Bitcoin chain serves as the underlying asset for its ETFs if the network forks. Should BlackRock, or another major holder such as MicroStrategy, back a chain that splits from the wider ecosystem, that could threaten both Bitcoin's value and its security.
