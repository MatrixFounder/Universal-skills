**Deliberation.** Bitcoin's only formal deliberation mechanism is the Bitcoin Improvement Proposal (BIP) process, moderated by Bitcoin Core developers on GitHub. Core developers do not decide unilaterally; extensive peer review shapes each BIP. Still, they hold a veto since the final call on integrating code is theirs.

Permissionlessness ranks among Bitcoin's core values, evident in the absence of any legal entity behind it. That structure yields autonomy at a cost — unlike many DAOs, Bitcoin cannot rely on a legal wrapper to interface with institutions. The user-activated-soft-fork mechanism carries a related danger: large economic actors could push Bitcoin in a new direction without the rest of the ecosystem's agreement. BlackRock, now among the largest Bitcoin holders, has written this authority into its contracts. Its iShares service agreement gives BlackRock the final say on which chain underlies its ETFs if Bitcoin forks. Should BlackRock and other major holders, MicroStrategy among them, back a chain that diverges from the wider ecosystem, Bitcoin's value and security could suffer significantly.
