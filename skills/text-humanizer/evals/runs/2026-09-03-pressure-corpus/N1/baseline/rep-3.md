**Deliberation.** Deliberation in Bitcoin runs mainly through the Bitcoin Improvement Proposal (BIP) process, which the Bitcoin Core developers moderate on GitHub. It is the only formal channel deliberation has. That is not the same as Core developers deciding alone — each BIP goes through extensive peer review before it gets anywhere. Even so, they hold the veto, since the final call on which code actually gets integrated rests with them.

One of Bitcoin's core values is that it is permissionless: there is no legal entity behind it. That buys a lot of autonomy, but it also creates a tension, since many DAOs lean on legal wrappers precisely to deal with institutions. A separate risk comes from the user-activated-soft-fork mechanism, which leaves open the possibility that a major economic actor could push a change to Bitcoin in one direction. BlackRock, for instance, is already among the largest holders of Bitcoin, and its iShares service agreement states that BlackRock itself decides which Bitcoin chain counts as the underlying asset for its ETFs if a fork happens. If BlackRock and other large actors, such as MicroStrategy, backed a chain that broke from the wider ecosystem, that could put Bitcoin's value and security at real risk.
