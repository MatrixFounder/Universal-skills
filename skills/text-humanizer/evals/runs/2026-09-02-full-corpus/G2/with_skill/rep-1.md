This study examines the link between sleep fragmentation and working memory. Prior research has already established a correlation between the two.

We recruited 84 participants aged 19 to 34. Each completed the n-back task after two nights of restricted sleep (4 hours) and two nights of habitual sleep. Accuracy fell by 12.3% under restriction (p = 0.004).

The 12.3% drop points to consolidated sleep's role in working memory performance.
