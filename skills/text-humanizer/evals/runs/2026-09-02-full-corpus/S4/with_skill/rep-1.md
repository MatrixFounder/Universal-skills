Sable, on a Thursday in November. Eighty-four pounds for two, before wine.

The lamb neck had been in there for six hours and gave in the second a fork touched it — dark at the edges, soft everywhere else, the kind of dish that makes you stop talking for a minute. The bread showed up twenty minutes late. And cold.

This is what modern British cooking looks like when someone actually bothers. I just wish they'd remembered the bread.
