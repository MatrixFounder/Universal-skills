**Deliberation.** Bitcoin's formal deliberation runs almost entirely through the Bitcoin Improvement Proposal (BIP) process, moderated by Bitcoin Core developers on GitHub. No other formal channel exists. Core developers do not decide alone: each BIP goes through thorough peer review before anything moves forward. Even so, they retain a veto. The final decision on whether code gets merged rests with them.

One of Bitcoin's founding principles is permissionlessness: no legal entity stands behind it. That buys autonomy, but it creates a tension, since many DAOs rely on legal wrappers to deal with institutions. The user-activated-soft-fork mechanism leaves an opening for major economic actors to shift Bitcoin in one direction without the rest of the network's agreement. BlackRock, for instance, is now one of the largest Bitcoin holders. Its iShares service agreement gives BlackRock sole discretion over which Bitcoin chain underlies its ETFs if the network forks. Should BlackRock and other major holders, MicroStrategy among them, back a chain out of step with the rest of the ecosystem, that could put Bitcoin's value and security at serious risk.
