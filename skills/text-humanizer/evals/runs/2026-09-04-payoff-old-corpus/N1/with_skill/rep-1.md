**Deliberation.** Bitcoin's only formal mechanism for deliberation is the Bitcoin Improvement Proposal (BIP) process, moderated by Bitcoin Core developers on GitHub. Core developers do not decide unilaterally; extensive peer review shapes each BIP. Still, they hold a veto. They have the final say on which code gets merged.

One of Bitcoin's core values is its permissionless nature: no legal entity stands behind it. That grants high autonomy, though it comes at a cost, since many DAOs lean on legal wrappers to interact with institutions. The user-activated soft fork mechanism introduces a specific risk: major economic actors could change Bitcoin unidirectionally. BlackRock, already one of Bitcoin's largest holders, has built this into its iShares service agreement, specifying that the firm alone decides which chain counts as the underlying asset for its ETFs if a fork occurs. If BlackRock and other major holders (MicroStrategy, among others) back a chain the broader ecosystem rejects, Bitcoin's value and security bear the risk.
