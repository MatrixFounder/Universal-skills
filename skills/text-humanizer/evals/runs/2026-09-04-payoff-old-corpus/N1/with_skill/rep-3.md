**Deliberation.** Bitcoin's only formal mechanism for deliberation is the Bitcoin Improvement Proposal (BIP) process, moderated by the Bitcoin Core developers on GitHub. Core developers do not decide unilaterally; each BIP goes through extensive peer review before it moves forward. Still, they hold a veto. The final call on whether code gets integrated rests with them.

Bitcoin's permissionless nature, evident in the absence of any legal entity behind it, is one of its most basic values. That gives it a high degree of autonomy, but it creates a tension: many DAOs rely on legal wrappers just to interface with institutions. The user-activated-soft-fork mechanism carries a risk of its own: major economic actors could unidirectionally change Bitcoin. BlackRock, for instance, is already among Bitcoin's largest holders. Its iShares service agreement reserves the right to decide, in the event of a fork, which Bitcoin chain underlies its ETFs. If BlackRock and other major economic actors, such as MicroStrategy, settled on a chain out of step with the rest of the ecosystem, that could put Bitcoin's value and security at serious risk.
