**Deliberation.** The Bitcoin Improvement Proposal (BIP) process, moderated by Bitcoin Core developers on GitHub, is Bitcoin's only formal channel for deliberation. Core developers do not decide alone: substantial peer review shapes each BIP. But they still hold a veto — in the end, developers decide which code gets integrated.

Bitcoin's permissionless nature is one of its core values, evident in the absence of any legal entity behind it. This yields high autonomy, but it creates a tension: many DAOs rely on legal wrappers to interface with institutions. The user-activated-soft-fork mechanism risks letting major economic actors change Bitcoin unidirectionally. BlackRock, for instance, already ranks among the largest Bitcoin holders; its iShares service agreement states that it decides which Bitcoin chain underlies its ETFs if the network forks. If BlackRock and other major actors (e.g., MicroStrategy) chose a chain out of step with the wider ecosystem, this could seriously threaten Bitcoin's value and security.
